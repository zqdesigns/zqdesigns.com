Why it matters

A lot of people learn electronics on their own: students filling the gaps their courses left, hobbyists who got curious, technicians moving up, and engineers picking up a field they never studied. They shouldn't have to choose between resources that cost too much and free ones that are thin, outdated, or just wrong.

In engineering, a confusing explanation isn't a small problem. It's like a crooked foundation: everything you build on top of it inherits the flaw. Free can't be an excuse for careless. Every lesson should be accurate, clear, and built around how people actually learn.

That's what we aim for with CircuitBread, and it's a big part of why I went back to school to study how engineering is taught.

CircuitBread

CircuitBread is a free place to learn electronics, from a first circuit to the topics an engineer looks up mid-project. It began as an in-house project at SwellFox, the Boise studio where I'm CEO and engineering lead. The team there bootstrapped it, built it from the ground up, and launched it publicly in January 2023.

Today it has more than 250 video tutorials, along with written lessons, study guides, an equation reference, a glossary, dozens of calculators and tools, and free online textbooks. It's all free, with no banner ads. The Friends of CircuitBread program partners with companies that offer learners something useful instead.

Much of my work is on the engineering side, helping set the standards our engineers and writers follow so lessons stay accurate and consistent. Right now we're organizing the lessons into structured courses, built on a map of how the core topics of electrical engineering connect, so learners can see what to study next and why.

CleverLabs

Since the beginning of 2026, CircuitBread has operated under CleverLabs, Inc., a 501(c)(3) nonprofit I helped found. CleverLabs develops and distributes free engineering education, and its mission is simple: well-crafted, free engineering resources for everyone. CircuitBread is where that work happens today.

Research

I'm also a PhD candidate in Instructional Design and Technology at Idaho State University. My dissertation connects cognitive psychology and engineering education, with a framework to help engineers, as subject-matter experts, design self-paced courses that teach well. I presented the framework, “From Instructional Design Theory to Practice,” at the AECT 2026 Design & Development Showcase in Chicago.
